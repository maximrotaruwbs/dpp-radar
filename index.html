04 - Scope

Who this actually applies to

The Digital Product Passport isn't only a "manufacturer problem." It touches every role that touches the product, from raw material to end of life.

🏭

Manufacturers & brand owners

Must generate the product data, attach a data carrier (QR/NFC/RFID), and register the passport in the EU DPP Registry before placing a product on the market.

📦

Importers & distributors

Responsible for verifying a valid passport exists before goods manufactured outside the EU enter the single market.

🛒

Retailers & marketplaces

Need to surface the passport (physically or online) at the point of sale, wherever the product is displayed to a shopper.

🔧

Repairers & recyclers

Rely on passport data - materials, disassembly, spare parts - to repair, refurbish or recover a product correctly at end of life.

🏛️

Customs & market surveillance

Use the Registry to check compliance at the border and during market checks, without opening a single box.

🙋

Everyday consumers

Scan the code on the product to see durability, repairability and environmental data before - or after - they buy.

05 - Mechanics

How a Digital Product Passport works

One data set, one unique identifier, many doors in.

The manufacturer's data + the physical data carrier both point to the same record in the Registry. Anyone with the right access level - a shopper, an inspector, a recycler - reaches it from a single scan.

08 - FAQ

Questions we keep getting

What exactly is a Digital Product Passport?
A structured, machine-readable set of data about a specific product - materials, origin, durability, repairability, carbon footprint, end-of-life instructions - linked to a unique identifier and reachable via a data carrier such as a QR code.
Is a DPP mandatory for my product right now?
It depends on the category. Batteries above 2kWh become mandatory from 18 February 2027. Other categories (textiles, tyres, furniture, iron & steel, aluminium…) follow through 2027–2030 as their delegated acts are adopted. Check the categories section above for the current status of your product group.
Who is legally responsible for creating it?
The economic operator placing the product on the EU market - typically the manufacturer or, for imported goods, the importer. Retailers and distributors must ensure a valid passport is available, not create one themselves.
Does a DPP replace my existing product labels?
No. It complements mandatory physical labelling (safety marks, care labels, energy labels) rather than replacing it. Think of it as a deeper, always-current layer sitting behind the label.
Where does the data actually live?
The full data set can be hosted by the manufacturer or a service provider; the EU DPP Registry stores the unique identifier and core metadata needed to look up and verify the record, not necessarily every field.
What happens if we don't comply in time?
Non-compliant products can be blocked from entering the EU market or withdrawn from sale by market surveillance authorities, alongside penalties set by each member state. Enforcement generally begins 18 months after a category's delegated act takes effect.
Is this page an official EU source?
No - DPP Radar is an independent aggregator built to make the public information easier to scan. Always confirm requirements that affect compliance decisions directly on ec.europa.eu.
